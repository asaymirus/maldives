Diving
The house reef is where it starts. From there, the North Ari Atoll opens into a world of thilas, channels and drifting current.
The North Ari Atoll holds some of the Maldives’ most storied dive sites: thilas thick with grey reef sharks, channels where mantas pass through on the current, a wreck slowly becoming its own reef. Most are within the hour by boat, run through Noovilu’s small dive team on the island’s own dhoni. Whether you’re taking your first breaths underwater or chasing your next certification, this is diving built around your pace, not a schedule.
Marine Life
For new divers, the water around our house reef is gentle enough to learn in, and rich enough to make it worth the effort. Turtles are near-constant company from your first dive, unbothered by bubbles and slow enough to swim alongside, while anemones and reef fish crowd every ledge. Reef sharks keep their distance, and the coral does the rest, drawing in colour and movement before you’ve found your buoyancy.
Venture into the atoll and the encounters scale up. Maaya Thila and Fish Head draw big schools of fusiliers, batfish and trevally, with nudibranchs and frogfish rewarding a closer look. From November to April, Moofushi Manta Point keeps a standing appointment, rays circling its cleaning station on schedule.
DIVING & Courses
DIVING & Courses
TRY SCUBA
No boat, no certification, no experience needed. Your first breaths underwater happen right off the house reef, guided every step by a Noovilu instructor. It’s a two-hour taste of diving, low-pressure enough for total beginners and honest enough to hook you.
LEARN TO DIVE
Open Water certification starts gently, in the shelter of the house reef, before opening out into real dives with turtles, rays and reef sharks for company. Structured around your stay rather than a fixed schedule, it’s a proper qualification without the rush.
GO DEEPER
Already certified? Advanced and specialty courses take you deeper: night diving, wreck diving, underwater photography, or a closer look at the sharks and mantas you’ve already met. Five dives, one instructor, and a reason to come back better qualified.

DIVE YOUR WAY
However you like to dive, there’s a way to do it here. Noovilu keeps the schedule flexible, whether you want a steady daily rhythm, something built around a specific sighting, or the whole boat kept just for you and yours.
DAILY DIVING
Two tanks in the morning, one in the afternoon, a steady rhythm built around the atoll’s best-known sites. It’s the easiest way to rack up dives without overthinking the calendar, with the boat leaving on the same schedule most days.
SPECIAL ADVENTURES
Night dives, early starts before the current picks up, full-day safaris further into the atoll. For the diver chasing something specific, whether that’s a manta season sighting, a wreck at dusk, or a site the daily rotation doesn’t usually reach.
PRIVATE DIVING
Your own guide for the day, or the whole Hammerhead to yourselves, arranged directly with Noovilu. Ideal for families diving together at their own pace, couples wanting the reef without a crowd, or anyone certifying somewhere quieter than a group class.

BOAT
Noovilu’s dive boat is the dhoni Hammerhead, home base for the two-tank morning dive and single afternoon dive that anchor the daily schedule. Night dives, early starts and full-day safaris are all built in whenever you want them.
EQUIPMENT
Full rental kit on hand, from BCDs and regulators to dive computers and tanks, so you can travel light or bring your own gear. Everything is checked and ready before each dive, no setup required on your part.
NITROX
Available on request for certified divers, with a small additional fee per fill. Enriched air means longer, safer bottom times on the deeper sites, and it’s worth asking about if you’re diving multiple days in a row.
FACILITIES
EFR-trained guides run every dive to Maldivian diving regulations, with full safety equipment on-site and a rinse and storage area for your gear. Everything you need is a short walk from your villa.
DIVE SITES
Here are nine of our favourites, though Noovilu’s reach extends well beyond them. There’s a gentle drift over Kandolhu’s own reef, a wreck slowly becoming a home for soft coral, and channels where grey reef sharks gather by the dozen, all within an hour’s boat ride, with plenty more across the atoll still to explore.
Dive Sites
HOUSE REEF
The island’s own reef, right off the beach and open any time you like. It rings Kandolhu completely, teeming with turtles, reef sharks and colour, no boat required.
BLUE CAVES
Named for its overhangs, draped in blue and violet soft coral. Grey reef sharks and blue-striped snapper move through in numbers; a favourite with photographers.
HAFZA THILA
A cleaning station where grey reef sharks gather in numbers, often close enough to watch the cleaner fish at work.
MAAYA THILA
One of the Maldives’ best-known sites, day or night. Expect eagle rays, barracuda schools, nudibranchs and the occasional guitar shark.
FISH HEAD
A protected marine reserve and one of the atoll’s signature dives, home to resident grey reef sharks patrolling the reef edge.
FESDU WRECK
A sunken trawler resting upright at 20 metres, now a reef in its own right, thick with soft coral and reef fish.
MOOFUSHI MANTA POINT
A channel dive at its best from December to April, when manta rays glide through alongside grey reef sharks and eagle rays.
DONKALO THILA
A second manta cleaning station beyond Moofushi, where rays hold steady in the current while cleaner fish patiently tend to their skin and gills.
ORIMAS THILA
Also known as Maaga Thila, after the island to its north, a hundred-metre reef with a striking northern face, explored best as a drift dive.
