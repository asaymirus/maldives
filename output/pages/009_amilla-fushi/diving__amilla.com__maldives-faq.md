Before Arrival
Amilla Maldives Resort & Residences, located in the otherworldly Baa Atoll of the Maldives, is within the UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. The Maldives is a tropical archipelago in Asia, straddling the equator. The island nation is surrounded by the Laccadive Sea (part of the Indian Ocean) and lies south of India and southwest of Sri Lanka.
The best way to travel to the Maldives is by air, although there are also provisions for international yacht arrivals. Abundant scheduled and charter flights operate between the Maldives and the rest of the world. Learn more on our
Getting Here
page and chart your journey to Amilla Maldives from any corner of the world.
All travellers receive a free 30-day visa upon arrival providing their passport is valid for at least six months. They also need a valid return air ticket home/onwards to another destination and a confirmed reservation at a resort.
The Maldives has a hot tropical climate, with plenty of sunshine, high humidity, and a constant sea breeze. The temperature generally hovers around 30°C (86°F) year-round, with night-time lows sometimes of 25°C (77°F). Sea temperatures are around 22 to 28° C (72–82°F). The climate is influenced by monsoons—the dry Northeast Monsoon from November to April, while the Southwest Monsoon (May–October) is technically the wet season. However, it’s very possible to have many sunny days, even during the rainy season.
The Maldives is a year-round destination, with a tropical climate and temperatures averaging 30°C (86°F) throughout the year. While snorkelling and diving have good visibility throughout the year, the Southwest monsoon season (May–October) is the ideal season to sight
manta rays in Hanifaru Bay
, the dry Northeast monsoon season (November–April) is when we see the clearest skies.
Malé’s Velana International Airport is accessible from various hubs around the world. From there, you can get to Amilla Maldives by a 35-minute shared or private seaplane or 20-minute domestic flight, followed by a 10-minute boat transfer. Transfer options are booked by the property with our partner Manta Airways.

As the Maldives islands are so remote, we have no large mammals such as monkeys or big cats, but we do have hectares of glorious jungle! Our jungle has a fabulous diversity of wildlife, including rare birds that live and breed in our ancient banyan trees. As with all Maldives islands, there is the occasional fruit bat and small lizard—as well as butterflies and other small insects (but nothing dangerous or of an Amazonian/Australian size!).
The Maldives is a very safe country for its wildlife. The majority of our country’s wildlife is found in the ocean. We urge you to avoid touching any ocean wildlife or coral as some species can sting or cause a rash (and you could harm them too by touching them). If you are concerned about hazards, the biggest concerns in the Maldives are sunburn or falling coconuts. In fact, we regularly send our best climbing experts to shimmy up coconut trees in our public areas and remove coconuts—so they won’t unexpectedly crash to the ground. Don’t worry—we don’t waste a thing. We have a coconut machine to make our own virgin coconut oil.
We also have pest control measures to help control mosquitoes etc. We encourage our guests to keep their doors and windows closed to ensure their villas and residences remain deliciously cool (as opening them affects the efficacy of the air-conditioning) and also helps circumvent any issues with ‘new friends’ that may be outside wanting to move in!
